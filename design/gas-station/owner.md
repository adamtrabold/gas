## 4. The arrow-sign article the owner mentioned (found in the owner's saved items)

Not the owner's words; a summary of what he saved. He was "not sure" it was there; this is the closest match.

- "The History Behind the Signs Lighting Up Our Daily Lives: Vacuum-Form Signage and Human Connection" — Beth Mathews, Substack.
  https://bethmathews.substack.com/p/the-history-behind-the-signs-lighting
  Saved summary: vacuum-formed (thermoformed) plastic signs came out of WWII manufacturing and changed sign design in the 1950s; Conrad Escalante and Kozy Boren of Superior Outdoor Display Co. made affordable, mass-produced lit signs for small businesses; signature products include the flashing "Superior arrow" and standard models such as the Dynalite and Big Sig; described as folk art that creates small human connections in neighbourhoods.
- Also saved: the Flickr group "Vacuum Formed Signs" (photo pool of vintage dimensional plastic signs). https://www.flickr.com/groups/vacuumformedsigns/pool/

## 9. The owner's response to Left on at 9 — SENT BACK (verbatim)

He was shown Left on (director's score 9), the before/after pairs, the sheet, and walked the refreshed round-3 prototype on his phone. His words:

"It's looking pretty pixellated and when I press and hold it often brings up the copy paste tooltip on mobile. I think we need more detail boss texturing and shadow methods like crosshatching etc it looks posterized"

Orchestrator's notes, not his words:
- This is a rejection of a 9. It is about the look and the prototype's behaviour; he said nothing against the idea (shadow / lamp / left on) or the place.
- "detail boss texturing" was a typo for "detail via texturing" — see section 10.
- Two of the points are defects in the prototype on a real phone: it renders pixellated, and press-and-hold (which is how you walk) brings up the phone's copy/paste tooltip.
- Earlier words that still stand unless he says otherwise: "pure ink for now"; strokes "readable but not precise or always whole"; "Ed Ruscha meets Jack Kirby's sketches"; and after the round-2 walk: "pretty high contrast… hard to really make out objects… more detail and variation in the brush stroke size".

## 10. Clarifications after sending it back (verbatim)

"Detail via* texturing. Two values but we can still make the illusion of grey. It's just a black and white painting."

(So section 9 reads: "we need more detail via texturing and shadow methods like crosshatching etc". Two values stands: black and white only, with grey as an illusion made by marks. His phrase for the whole thing: "a black and white painting".)

## 11. A further idea from the owner (verbatim)

"We could also leverage different paper textures for the forms"

(Offered as an option — "could" — alongside texturing and crosshatching.)

Asked whether the paper textures stay within black and white or a form could sit on differently toned paper, he said: "Within black and white, different grain showing through the ink"

He then described the method he has in mind (verbatim): "Yeah think like if I'd scanned in various paper textures, made them greyscale and multiplied onto the vintage color, and then applied them to each face / element"

(Orchestrator's note, not his words: "the vintage color" suggests the paper/ground has a vintage tone rather than pure white; and a greyscale scan multiplied on is, literally, continuous tone in the grain. How that sits with "two values… the illusion of grey" is for the look test to show him, not for us to decide.)
