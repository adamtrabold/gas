# 02 — The Arrow

*How it works (systems). Read `00-shared.md` first.*

The arrow sign points at the station. Its shadow points at something else, and what it points at changes all day. Whatever the shadow is on is awake.

**Draws on:** the director's "The Instrument"; the sundial (jam, director pass 1; systems pass 2); the raking beam (visual pass 2); the star clock for night (systems pass 2).

**Kept:** a real sun with no cheats; a full hand in every season; the station stays the subject; it has to work for someone who never works out that it's a dial. Twilight points at nothing.
**Changed:**
- The beam was a leaning pylon on a streamlined station. Now it is the owner's Googie arrow sign on a plain Route 66 station: a long arrow raked over the forecourt, tail at the road, head toward the building, his name along the shaft. An arrow is the one shape whose shadow is unmistakably a pointer.
- The hour chose a piece of work. There is no work yet, so **the hour chooses a thing.**
- Painted hour lines made the lot a diagram (the director's objection). The lines are now the expansion joints of a concrete forecourt, which every old station has. Nobody painted a dial. It's just how the slab cracked.
**Dropped:** the timetable; the high camera; a real latitude (a generic desert one is used; "nowhere in particular").

This is the concept for "yes, the hour should do something."

## Structure

Six stops. The same line as 01, with the forecourt doing more and the inside doing less.

```
road → forecourt → [door] → shop → [back door] → back lot → road
```

**The forecourt is laid out by the sun.** You arrive from the road looking north; the day's shadows sweep left to right in front of the building. Seven things stand where the joints run:

| About | The arrow's shadow is on |
|---|---|
| Early morning | The ice box, far left |
| Mid morning | The west pump |
| Late morning | The air hose and bell line |
| Noon | The door |
| Early afternoon | The window |
| Mid afternoon | The east pump |
| Evening | The phone box, far right, then off down the road |

**Inside, the hand is the patch of sun through the window.** It crosses the floor and the counter the other way, and whatever it lies on is the awake thing indoors. Outside the hand is ink on paper; inside it's paper on ink.

## What "awake" means

Every thing answers every hour. The awake one does more:

- It makes its sound without being touched: the pump ticks, the ice box drips, the phone box hums.
- Look, Call or Pick up gives its fuller answer, the one it only has at its hour.
- It is the only thing in the forecourt with solid blacks you didn't put there.

Someone who never notices the pattern sees a big arrow lying on the ground with its head on a pump, and the pump ticking. That is enough. Someone who comes back at four sees it on the other pump.

Nothing is locked: the fuller answer is an extra, and the wall clock reaches all of them.

## Night

The sun is gone, so the arrow lights itself: bulbs chase down the shaft to the head (the "Superior arrow" in the owner's saved article flashed). **By day the sun moves the hand. At night the arrow is its own light and points one way only: at the door.** The pool of paper at the door is the awake thing all night.

The sky turns round the arrow's tip, each star a stroke with a tail; the length of the tails is how old the night is. For now the stars are only a clock. When there's work, they have a use.

## Twilight

For half an hour at each end nothing is awake. The shadow has run off down the road and the bulbs aren't on. If something odd happens, it happens now.

## The wall clock

Turn the hand indoors and through the window the arrow's shadow swings across the forecourt, waking each thing in turn; the sun patch crosses the room the other way. One control shows you everything the place does. Marked as wound; lets go when you leave.

## The gestures here

| Gesture | Note |
|---|---|
| Look | The awake thing already has its blacks; Look gives them to anything else for a moment |
| Cross | The latch. At noon the shadow is on the door and it stands ajar: the one hour with no latch |
| Pick up | Shop things, as 01 |
| Call | Bell line, pump handle, phone box. Fuller at their hour |
| Wait | The shadow moves. Stay ten minutes and you'll see it leave one thing for the next |

## The alien material

Alignments. On the shortest day the arrowhead's shadow reaches the threshold at noon and at no other time in the year. Other dates put it on other marks. And one piece of evidence that fits only here: now and then there is **a second shadow** on the forecourt, at the wrong angle for the sun, with nothing above it.

## Adam makes it into something

A new thing outdoors takes a place on a joint, which gives it an hour. A new thing indoors takes a place the sun patch crosses. About nine outdoors and nine indoors before two share an hour; after that, new things go where the hand doesn't reach and are simply always the same.

## States particular to this concept

- **Midsummer noon:** the raked arrow still casts a full hand. No stretching.
- **Winding at night:** the sun comes up outside the window while it's dark where you are. The margin says so.
- **No 3D:** one sentence does the work of the dial — the time, and what the arrow is on.
- **Motion off:** the shadow steps from joint to joint.

## What it has to prove

That the arrow can be raked over the forecourt and still be a Googie arrow sign and not a piece of apparatus. That "awake" is felt without a word of explanation. That the inside holds up when most of the idea is outside.

## Not sure about

- Whether a Route 66 arrow sign ever leaned like this. Visual should check it against the owner's saved sign references before we fall in love with it.
- Seven fuller answers outdoors and seven indoors is fourteen things to make well. It's the most work of the four.
