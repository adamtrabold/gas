## The owner's objections

**The one he has actually made:** "Rendering is pretty high contrast though ... hard to really make out objects. I think there can be slightly more detail and variation in the brush stroke size or something to give more detail to the scene."

Would he still say it, looking at this build?

- **Much less.** In the look he walked, a pump in sun was blank and a pump in shade was a slab. Now a pump is a hatched box with an island under it and a circle for its globe (`lo-pumps-a` against `-b`). There is a bench that could not be seen at all before (`lo-front-b`). Indoors the stock, the things on the counter and the cooler are separate things (`lo-room-b`). A pump at night under the lamp is drawn (`lo-nightpump-b`).
- **"Variation in the brush stroke size"** is met directly: road, ground, walls, things and small things each wear the strokes at their own size, and I can count four or five sizes in one frame.
- **"Slightly more" is met, not overshot.** It is one clear step. The place is still mostly empty paper by day and mostly ink at night.
- **Where he might still say it:** under the canopy at high noon the pump body is still a slab (`lo-shade-b`, which the designer calls the weakest pair), and at night anything outside your lamp is not there, which is the concept. I'd tell him both.
- **"High contrast"** can't change while it is two values, and two values is his own "pure ink for now". If the pairs don't satisfy him, the next step is a question for him about grey, not more lines.

**Other objections he may raise, none of which I think is likely enough to hold it back:**

1. **"The outlines are thin and even."** The new edge line is a hairline. Up close on the building (`lo-walk-6s`, `lo-front-b`) it reads a little like a line render and not like a brush. At dusk, with no shadows to carry the drawing, the place is mostly these lines and is the nearest it comes to a wireframe. He asked for this kind of detail and attached Kirby's line art, so I expect him to welcome it; but Kirby's line has weight. See fix 1.
2. **"What's wrong with the pole?"** At dusk the sign pole is a ladder of small lines. Known flaw; he will see it within seconds of `#dusk`.
3. **"It's silent."** He said "we need sound effects at least". Sound is designed and nothing is audible. He needs telling before he opens it.
4. **"The strokes look generated."** They do. His own sheet is one file. He chose "generate for now".
5. **"The building redraws as I walk up."** The far strokes hand over to fine ones between 32 m and 12 m. Nobody has seen that in motion on a screen. Only his eye can judge it.

## Rulings the designers asked for

- **The edgeless wall.** I ruled to keep "a sunlit wall against the sky has no edge". The visual designer broke it and chose the owner's eye. They were right and my ruling is withdrawn. He told us he couldn't make things out; a rule that hides the building from the person who owns it isn't worth keeping. From the road the building is still drawn only by its shade, which is where the Ruscha matters.
- **The shadow.** A person with a clean outline, the one mark kept cleaner than the brush. Yes. One thing to tidy: indoors, under the room's own lights, the mark at your feet is a ragged tuft that reads as an animal (`lo-room-b`, `lo-aisle`). Give it the same clean rule or don't draw it there.
- **The far-stroke handover.** Leave it on for his walk. It is the direct answer to "variation in the brush stroke size", it holds frame to frame, and whether it reads as coming into focus or as crawling is exactly the kind of thing his eye settled last time in one sentence. Keep `?big=0` ready.

## Before the link goes to him

Small, and none needs re-scoring. The orchestrator can check them.

1. **Give the edge line a brush's weight.** Thicker where it turns a corner or sits in shade, thinner and broken in the open, and fewer of them: silhouettes and main corners, not every edge. Most needed at dusk. If there isn't time, tell him the line is a first pass.
2. **The pole at dusk.** Remove the ladder.
3. **A black frame by day.** In my `#day` walk, standing at the foot of the sign after seven seconds of walking, a turn of about 40° gave one frame that was solid ink. I think the view was inside or hard against the pole. Check you can't walk into the sign.
4. **The tuft at your feet indoors** (above).
5. **Standing still at night**, my frame after 12 seconds was solid ink with no stars in it, because I was facing away from the still point and level. That may be correct. Check that someone who stops at night always has something: stars in frame, or the station's lights, or the pool not fully closing.

## What the owner should be told when he sees it

1. **What it is.** One concept, Left on: you are never drawn; you're a shadow where the sun is and a lamp where it isn't; whatever you switch on stays on. A station somebody stepped away from.
2. **What he can do in the prototype:** arrive by day, dusk and night; walk the lot, go in, walk the aisle and out the back; walk off into the desert and arrive again; carry the lamp indoors and at night.
3. **What is designed and not in it yet:** all sound; switches switching; the door's crossing stroke; the bell; the place doing things when you stand still; lights kept between visits; the chart; most of the alien material.
4. **What changed because of what he said:** things have a tone of their own, strokes come in more sizes, lost edges get a line, day shade is more open. Show him the five pairs. Ask: is that enough, too much, or the wrong kind of detail?
5. **What only he can judge:**
   - Walking up to the station by day, do the marks on the building come into focus, or crawl?
   - Do the thin edge lines feel like part of the drawing?
   - Is his shadow his?
6. **What is his to replace:** every stroke (one sheet), every shape (Blender models), every sound.

## If he isn't satisfied: the questions that would unblock it

Not needed for the score; ready if his answer to the pairs is "still not it".

1. Still hard to make out things: would he allow one grey, or should it stay pure ink and get more line?
2. The edge lines: heavier and fewer like Kirby's, or gone from the building and kept on objects only?
3. The strokes: would he draw one sheet of his own now, so we stop tuning a generator he will replace?

## What blocked a 9 across the rounds, for the record

- Round 1: no brush strokes in anything walkable.
- Round 2: no desert, a blank day arrival, a refused GAS blade, an undrawn dusk.
- Both: nobody had held it.

The look ran one round behind the idea each time, because each round's visual effort went, rightly, to the hardest technical question first. What unblocked it this round was the owner walking it. One sentence from him did more than my two rounds of objections, and it was an objection I had not made: I had been checking for brush and for his words, and he looked at it and couldn't tell what things were. In the build, get a walkable thing in front of him early and often.
