# Sheet 1 judge — design director

A check of Sheet 1 (`stills/sheet-1-pump.png`, `sheet-1.md`) before it goes to the owner. Not a score. Includes `owner.md` section 16.

## Verdict: fix first, a short list, then send

The sheet is the best thing this project has produced and it is the right form. Six small changes first, most of them edits to the sheet and the link. None needs me to look again.

## How I checked

- The sheet at page size, then cut into five pieces and read at full size.
- Beside his four flash sheets, the Kirby boards and the brush eye.
- His Route 66 photo, cropped to one pump and enlarged, against pump A1 part by part.
- **The walked pump, opened myself** in Playwright's iPhone 14 emulation at 3 dots per point (canvas 1170 × 1992 confirmed): default, `cut`, `dot`, `tall`, `fixed`, `night`, `dot-night`, a frame on arrival and one after holding to walk.
- Limits unchanged: headless, stills, no real touch, no motion.

## What's right

- It reads as flash. Line first, tone made of marks, things alone on old paper, captions typed. Next to the woodcut sheet and the candlestick sheet it belongs in the room.
- Against "posterized": no. The cabinet is built from many short strokes and stipple, rust rises from the base in blotches, lettering is flecked. I can't find a frame on the sheet he would call posterized.
- Against "pixellated": no, at full size. Edges are clean.
- No clock. Every part I checked against his Route 66 photo is there in the photo: globe, grade plate, the round knob under it, the three-row number window, hose from the side, dark base. The designer left out weeds because they aren't on the list. Correct, and my steer was wrong to ask for them.
- It is honest. The "Plainly" block is on the sheet.
- "Abandoned" is drawn in: rust, cracked globe, dusty glass, worn print.

## What he would catch in one glance

In the order I think he'd say them.

1. **"The one I can walk round doesn't look like this."** This is the big one. Every flash view on the sheet uses the "drawn once" light. The link opens on "lit by the hour". I opened it: the sunlit face of the walked pump is nearly bare, with a scatter of spots on it, and the shaded side is a dark ladder. That is two zones again, which is what he called posterized. The sheet says it flatters by lens and side. It does not say it flatters by light, and light is most of the difference. With `fixed` on, the walked pump is much closer to the sheet.
2. **"PLAYA?"** To a lot of people that word is slang before it is a dry lake. On a site that has to be "liminal not funny", the name on the largest lettering on the sheet can't have a second reading. He has now said of brand names: "propose". So propose properly.
3. **"B1 and B2 are the same."** At phone size both are vertical streaks on a cabinet. And B1 is labelled BRUSH and doesn't look brushed; the designer says it reads as wood grain, and it does. He asked for brush strokes in his first message. Offering him "brush" that isn't one will cost that option unfairly.
4. **"What is that?"** C5, the hose and nozzle. It is a square of stripes. The sheet admits it. A drawing we know doesn't read shouldn't be one of six things we show.
5. **"D1 and D2 are the same picture."** They are, at this size, because his own shadow covers the pump in both afternoon frames. We'd be asking him a question the pictures don't show.
6. **"That's not the pump in my photo."** A2 says "from your 1934 station photo" and is the short pump made taller. And A1's top is a dome on a neck, where the pump in his Route 66 photo has a flat top with soft corners and a front panel standing proud of the sides. Which shape is the question we're asking, so this is fair to show, but the captions claim more than the drawings deliver.

Not catches, but noted: the rust blotches at the base are heavy and read a little like spilled ink in B1 and B2. The specks of paper in the night sky of D read as snow. Lettering is type, and the sheet says so.

## Is the flattering light fair?

Fair on the sheet, since flash shades a thing for itself and the sheet is asking whether he wants that. Not fair if the link then opens on something plainer without a word. Fix 1 makes it fair.

## Does anything read as funny?

Only PLAYA. The title "No. 1 The Pump", the sun on the globe, NO SMOKING STOP MOTOR CONTAINS LEAD, the price of 19: all flat and plausible.

## Section 16, and what it changes on this sheet

His words: "This is liminal space logic so abandoned can still have power. Maybe someone's squatting." Brand names: "propose". The made-up items: "sounds good".

- **Power is on.** A pump globe is a lamp. At night the globe should be lit, cracked or not. In the night frames it already looks that way. Mark it on the sheet as following from his words, and add it to the pump's entry in `elements.md`. It is the pump's own piece of "left on".
- **The numbers still roll.** Already on the list; now it has his reason.
- **"Maybe someone's squatting"** changes nothing on the pump. It matters for the room. Don't put signs of a person on the pump.
- **The crack, the dust and the perished hose** were PROPOSED and he has said "sounds good". Change those tags.
- **Brands: propose.** See fix 2.

The sign (neon, historically accurate to his article, some letters flickering) is the next sheet and I haven't judged anything for it here.

## Fix first

1. **Match the link to the sheet.** The walked pump opens in the same light as the flash views (drawn once), in the hand shown first on the sheet. Add one line to "Plainly": the big drawings use the pump's own shading; switch to "lit" to see it shaded only by the sun.
2. **Replace PLAYA, and propose names in words.** Put three or four candidate names in a line on the sheet for him to choose from, each checked by someone against real oil brands first. One of them on the globe as a stand-in, plainly labelled as one. Keep LLANO only if it passes the same check.
3. **Make B a real choice.** Either B1 looks like a brush at a glance (fewer, broader strokes that curve with the form and run dry), or withdraw it and say on the sheet "brush isn't ready; choose between cut and dot for now". Don't leave two near-identical options with different names.
4. **Take C5 off the sheet.** Say in "Plainly" that the nozzle isn't drawn yet. Five good parts beat six with one that doesn't read.
5. **Make D show its difference, or drop it.** Use the mid-morning pair, where "lit by the hour" is visibly barer than "drawn once". If the difference still can't be seen at phone size, take D off and ask it later, walked.
6. **Captions that claim what's drawn.** A1 "short, round-shouldered, after your Route 66 photo". A2 "taller, stepped top, loosely after your 1934 photo; not yet matched to it". Update the PROPOSED tags he has accepted, and mark the lit globe.

If 3 or 5 can't be done quickly, take the honest fallback in each and send.

## What to ask him, in plain words

1. **Which pump?** The short round-shouldered one, or the taller one with the stepped top. And are your two pumps the same shape or one of each?
2. **Which way of drawing?** Even line with short hatching, or stipple. (And brush, if it's on the sheet.)
3. **Is this what you meant by flash sheet vibes?** Each thing drawn alone on old paper like this.
4. **Should a thing keep its own shading at every hour, with shadows and night added on top? Or should the sun and your lamp do all the shading, so it looks barer in full sun?**
5. **Paper:** one old sheet behind everything with two values only, or a scanned paper on each part, which puts a soft grey into the steel?
6. **Which name?** From the short list on the sheet, or your own.
7. **On your phone:** is it sharp, is it still fluid, and does press and hold only walk now?

## Where I disagree with myself

Last time my pick was brush on scanned paper. Having seen the three hands on one object, I've changed it; see the notes. The reason is the same one that has run through every round: the more line and mark there is, the closer it gets to what he sends us.
