## 1. What I see in the four sheets

**The woodcut sheet (19): figures, a snake in a ring round two clasped hands, stars.**
- A bold, even outline does nearly all the work. Tone is short parallel hatching that follows the form. No solid black anywhere. Most of each figure is bare paper.
- The paper is a thing in itself: aged, toned, with old blackletter text ghosting through from behind.
- One motif sits in a ruled frame with its own sun and ground. The rest float.
- A small caption in blackletter sits on the sheet like another drawing.

**The photocopy sheet (20): a candelabra, a ring of thorns, eyes, a skull.**
- Tone by **stipple**: dots packed closer for dark. And by damage: ink that has bled, dropped out, been copied too many times. Edges are rough because the print is worn, not because the drawing is loose.
- Different motifs are in different hands on one sheet: fine engraving line, coarse stamp, dot-screen.
- Lettering as an object: a sign-style italic, hatched, sitting among the drawings.
- Lots of empty paper between things.

**The soft pencil sheet (21): birds, a tiger, small figures.**
- Short strokes laid like fur or feathers. The mark is the material.
- Soft, grey, gentle. The quietest of the four, and the most whimsical.

**The brush-pen sheet (22): frogs, figures, a star with an eye in it.**
- A loose, fast brush-pen line with real weight. Solid blacks spotted in. A **dot screen** dropped in as a flat mid-tone, sometimes spilling past the outline.
- Every figure has a few strokes of its own ground under it. No shared floor, no shared light.
- Flecked, pulpy paper.

**What the four share**
1. **Each thing is drawn alone**, whole, from its best side, with air round it. A sheet is a set of separate complete drawings.
2. **Line first.** A confident contour says what the thing is. Tone comes second and is made of marks: hatching, stipple, dot screen, spotted black. That is "two values, the illusion of grey", exactly.
3. **No common light.** Shading belongs to the thing. Nothing casts a shadow on anything else.
4. **Old paper**, visible everywhere, with a life of its own.
5. **Lettering is drawn**, and sits on the sheet as a piece of flash.
6. **The print is worn.**

**And one thing we already had.** His UFO sightings chart, from the first batch, is the same form: separate small line drawings, each alone in a cell with a typed caption. He has been showing us this since the start. The starburst on top of the sign is a piece of flash already.

## What I would take, and what I would not

**Take**
- A thing is a complete drawing by itself before it is part of a scene. Line first, then two or three kinds of mark for tone, then paper.
- Stipple and dot tone alongside hatching.
- Each thing carrying a few marks of its own ground.
- The aged sheet as the paper of the whole world.
- Wear in the printing as the way "abandoned" is drawn: dropout, bleed, patchy ink, as well as rust and peeling on the thing.
- Drawn lettering. "Realistic labeling with fake brand names" is a sheet of flash by itself: badges, decals, grade plates, an oil can's label.

**Do not take** (he said "a bit", and "liminal not funny")
- The subjects: creatures, faces, gnomes, frogs, hearts, skulls, thorns, occult emblems. Our subjects are the things on the element list and nothing else. No mascots.
- Whimsy and charm. The pencil sheet's sweetness is the furthest thing from an abandoned station.
- A crowded page. The lot stays empty.
- Medieval or tattoo-parlour costume. No blackletter, no banners, no roses.
- Soft pencil grey. He has said two values.
- Any new motif on the station that isn't in his photos or on the list. The one place emblem-like marks belong is the alien material: the circles on the ground, the chart.

## How it fits "develop all the elements individually" and the list

It is the same idea. A flash sheet is a wall of separate drawings you choose from. The element list is the wall's contents. So:

- **Each element gets drawn as a piece of flash first:** alone, on paper, from its best side, with its parts from the list and nothing invented.
- **The sheets are the design record for the look.** One sheet per element or per family. When he agrees a sheet, that is how the thing is drawn.
- **A thing's best side is already in the concept:** every thing has a standing spot and a facing for when you tap to walk to it. That view is its flash view.
- It also suggests what the chart on the wall, the list of places, and the picture you get with no 3D could look like. Not for now.

## 2. Where his references pull apart

I'm naming these, not settling them. Each has something to show him.

**A. A thing with its own drawing, against a world where light makes the drawing.**
On a flash sheet a pump would be shaded once, by hand, and look the same at noon and at midnight. In our world ink is where the light isn't; the sun's shadows, your shadow and your lamp are the whole concept. Which wins on the surface of a thing?
*Show him:* the same pump twice, by day and in the lamp at night. Once with its drawing fixed and light only adding shade over it and revealing it in the dark. Once fully redrawn by the light as now. (The first is also what his own plan amounts to, drawing textures onto Blender models. But let him see it.)

**B. Brush, against pen, woodcut and stipple.**
His first words were "brush strokes" and he sent a brush-drawn eye. Kirby's boards are pen and brush line. Three of the four new sheets are line and dots; one is brush-pen. "A black and white painting" says brush. "Crosshatching" says pen.
*Show him:* one pump in three hands, side by side: loose brush-pen line with spotted blacks and a dot tone; even woodcut line with short hatching; fine line with stipple and a worn, copied look.

**C. One sheet of old paper, against a paper for each material.**
He described scanned papers applied to each face. Flash is one sheet behind everything.
*Show him:* both in one picture: the whole thing on one aged sheet, with the grain changing inside each material.

**D. Dots.**
Until now a regular row of dots meant bulbs and a loose cluster meant the alien material, and nothing else was dots. If stipple and dot screens become ordinary tone, that signal is gone. This one is yours to solve, not his: either dots stay rare and tone is hatching, or the alien material gets a different mark. Say which.

**E. Flat and frontal, against walked.**
Flash is drawn from one side. He walks round things. Yours to solve: the drawing has to hold from the sides nobody chose, and it has to stay on the thing as you move. The steps from the last test still apply.

**F. Charm.**
Two of the four sheets are funny. He said not funny. I've read "a bit" as the manner, not the mood. Question 1 below checks that.

## 3. Outcomes for the next piece of work

All of steer 3 still stands: tone in degrees made of marks, shadows that end where lines end, black last, marks that follow form, a texture per material, a line with weight, more to find up close, smooth at the pixel. Added now:

1. **Stands alone.** Any element, cut out and put on bare paper, is a complete drawing someone would choose off a wall. If it only works in the scene, it isn't done.
2. **Line carries it.** Cover the tone and the contour alone still says what the thing is.
3. **Nothing invented.** Every part is on `elements.md` with a source. The clock is the lesson. If drawing a thing needs a part that isn't listed, stop and add it to the list as PROPOSED.
4. **Abandoned is drawn in.** Rust, dents, a cracked globe, a perished hose, stucco fallen away to brick, weeds at the base, lettering half gone. And the print itself worn. It should look left, not ruined for effect.
5. **Lettering is drawn and believable.** Fake brand names that could have existed, in the manner of the period, made by a sign painter's hand. His name appears on the sign only.
6. **Still his place.** No faces, no creatures, nothing winking. Two values. The lot empty.
7. **Legible in shade against shade,** from the last test: a thing in shadow in front of a shadowed wall can still be told apart.
8. **Sharp on a phone at its real density,** checked zoomed in.

**With it**

**Why this, and not more**
- He asked for a list, then elements one at a time. The list is agreed. This is element one.
- The pump is the smallest thing with steel, glass, rubber and lettering on it, so it settles how a made object is drawn.
- It answers four things in one look: which pump, which hand, whether "flash" means what I think, and whether the list is working.
- A sheet is quick to judge on a phone, and it is the form he just asked for.
- After he picks: slab and island, then canopy, in the order in `elements.md`, each as a sheet.

I'd want to see Sheet 1 before it goes to him. I'll check it beside his four sheets, his Kirby boards and his brush eye, at phone size and zoomed in.
