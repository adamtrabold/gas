# Sheet 2 judge — design director

A check of Sheet 2 (`stills/sheet-2-island-canopy.png`, `sheet-2.md`) and the walked prototype before they go to the owner. Not a score. I am a new director; I started from `notes/director.md` and judged the work, not the earlier calls.

## Verdict: fix first, a short list, then send as a decision question labelled as a test

The pump is settled and good. The rule "dots for the thing, lines for the light" is a fair reading of his picks and should be put to him. But the frames that carry that question have faults we can already see, and two of them contradict what the sheet tells him. If he looks now, he will spend his look on those. Six fixes, three of them real work. I want to see four stills afterwards; no full re-judge.

## Against his photos and the list

- **Pump:** globe on a short neck, grade plate, round knob, three-row number window, hose from the side into a boot, dark base. All in his Route 66 photo. His pump is a box with rounded corners and a front panel standing proud; ours is rounder in plan. He picked ours from Sheet 1 by looking at it, so it stands.
- **Hose and nozzle:** reads as a nozzle on the pump. The close-up piece is the weakest drawing in section A but passes.
- **Slab and island:** joints, kerb, cracks, scrub, stains, sand, broken edge, bell line: all on the list.
- **Canopy:** flat slab, fascia, two thin posts at the outer corners, as in his abandoned-station photo. Joined to the building, as he said. Round plain posts where his photo's look square with a painted band: stated in `elements.md`, not on the sheet.
- **Lamps:** the list gives [1934] for "lamps underneath". The 1934 photo has glass globes on stems. Ours is an enamel dome, which is the shape of the lamp posts in his Route 66 photo. The caption should say so.
- **Not on the list and present:** the wording of the small notice on the pump (NO SMOKING / STOP MOTOR / CONTAINS LEAD) and the price. Plausible, not funny, ours. Tag PROPOSED.
- **Removed, rightly:** post feet and heads, the eave, the bucket.

## Against his words

- **"Pixellated":** no. Edges are clean at true density. Two small rendering faults: a white hairline along the horizon in every day frame, and stray white dashes in the black at night.
- **"Posterized":** no. There are no flat zones by day. If anything it has gone the other way; see below.
- **"Hard to really make out objects":** answered. Under the canopy the pumps stand clear of the shaded wall from every side I tried. From the east their dark sides are close stipple against a lined floor and they read at once.
- **"Detail via texturing and shadow methods like crosshatching":** this sentence is the best support for the designer's rule. He named two things: texture, and crosshatching as a way of drawing shadow. Dots and paper on the thing are the first. Lines for shade are the second.
- **"Two values… a black and white painting":** two values, yes (plus the paper's grey, which he chose). A painting, no. By day there is no solid black anywhere and almost no heavy line except on the pumps. The day frames read as a light pen drawing. The night frames have the black.
- **"A bit of flash sheet vibes":** the pump, alone, yes. The six slab pieces and the fascia and post pieces are not drawings anyone would choose off a wall; they are rectangles of texture.
- **"Liminal not funny":** nothing funny. One thing to know: the working lamp seen from directly below is a ring with a bright centre and reads as an eye.
- **Abandoned with the power on:** night under the one lamp is the best whole picture we have (`s2j-night-frontal.png`): a lit forecourt in a black field, globes on, windows black. By day "abandoned" is carried only by speckle. The geometry is new: straight joints, clean canopy.

## Beside his references

- **His stippled flash sheet:** his dots are very fine and even, and dark is made by packing them. Ours match that within arm's reach of the pump. From 3 m out ours are several times coarser against the thing and vary a lot in size, so they read as spatter or corrosion. On a pump that suits "abandoned". On the canopy it reads as a speckled ceiling.
- **His Kirby boards:** bold contour everywhere, spotted black, hatching that swells and tapers. Our pumps have the contour. The canopy, wall and slab are hairlines. Our shade lines are even, ruled and parallel. Night has the blacks; day has none.
- **His Route 66 photo:** stand in the road square on to the pumps (`s2j-day-frontal.png`) and our frame is recognisably that photograph drawn. It is the strongest day frame and it is not on the sheet. The sheet leads with a three-quarter view where the canopy's underside fills a third of the picture.

## What he would catch in one glance

1. **"I picked dots. Why is the pump drawn in lines?"** At night in his own lamp the cabinet is ruled top to bottom with vertical lines and the top half is solid black with white plates floating on it (`s2j-pump-night-your-lamp.png`). By day beside a pump under the canopy the "thin veil" is the same ruled lines over the whole cabinet (`s2j-day-near-pump-veil.png`). In both, the hand he passed over on Sheet 1 has come back on the thing itself. The sheet says a lamp "shows the drawing that was always there". For his own lamp, which is the centre of the concept, that isn't so.
2. **"Is that a wood floor?"** Where shade deepens on the slab the two families of lines meet in a regular chevron. Under the pumps it reads as herringbone parquet, and from the east a zigzag seam runs across the forecourt like a zip (`s2j-day-east-zigzag.png`). It is on the sheet, in the actual-pixels piece.
3. **"My shadow is a barcode."** Even vertical bars with clipped ends. It is one of only six slab pieces on the sheet and it is the concept's daytime half.
4. **Depending on the hour he opens it:** the link with no name after it opens on the lone pump, by his own clock. In the evening that is catch 1 as the first thing he sees.
5. **"Face each other?"** The words on the sheet don't match the picture or his photo.
6. **"The stripes?"** The fascia piece shows blotches. The sheet admits it, and still spends a piece on it.

## Does "dots for the thing, lines for the light" honour his picks?

Yes, with two breaks and one thing he has to be asked.

**Why it is fair.**
- On Sheet 1 the choice he said "no" to redrawing was captioned: the pump keeps its own shading at every hour; night and shadows are only added. Lines laid over are "added".
- His own sentence asked for texturing and for "shadow methods like crosshatching". That is this rule, in his words.
- He was asked about a pump on bare ground. Nobody asked him about the ground. Ground and walls have no shading of their own to keep, and the concept needs somewhere for his shadow and his lamp to show. So the ground is lit by the hour, openly. That is not the rejected option under another name: the rejected option was the sun shading the pump's own form.
- It keeps things apart from what is behind them, which was his first complaint.
- It settles what dots mean (matter) and leaves bulbs and the alien marks their own signs.

**Where it breaks his pick.**
- The veil is too much up close. A pump in shade stops being a dotted pump and becomes a hatched one.
- His own lamp at night does the same, more strongly.
- Both are tuning, not the rule. Fix 1.

**What only he can say.**
- In the walked day frame about two thirds of the ink is line. He picked dots, and the picture he walks into is mostly hatching. That may be exactly what he wants (texture on things, hatching for shadow) or it may not. It is the question.
- "No" might have meant less than we read into it. So ask in words that don't lean on Sheet 1.

**The other two ways.** Shade as more dots is purer to the pick and looks like dirt. Nothing changing at all leaves no shadow, no lamp and no hour. Show both small, as now, with neutral captions.

## Fix first

1. **A thing stays in dots, in every light.**
   - In his own lamp at night the pump shows its dots as it does under the canopy's lamp. Lines only where the pool ends on the ground.
   - In day shade the veil is light enough that the pump still reads as dotted from 1 m: a few broken lines, or none.
   - Then the sheet's sentence about lamps is true.
2. **Shade lines that look laid by a hand.**
   - No chevron, no herringbone, no zigzag seam where line families or surfaces meet. Deeper shade is a second set crossing the first at a shallow angle, uneven.
   - His shadow: lines of different length and weight with ragged ends, not bars.
3. **The link opens on the three together, by day, whatever his clock says.** Night is one tap away. Square on to the pumps from the road is the stand to open on.
4. **Shorten the sheet and lead with the strongest frame.**
   - Add the square-on view, day and night, at the top of "together".
   - Cut the slab to three pieces: the whole, the broken edge, his shadow. Cut the fascia and post pieces. Say in "Plainly" what was left off and why.
   - Target about 5,000 px tall.
5. **Ask the light question fairly, and without letters.**
   - Sheet 1's "D1" meant drawn once. Sheet 2's "D1" means something else, and he answers in letters. Name the three ways in words on the sheet: "lines for shade", "dots for shade", "nothing changes".
   - Captions say what each is, not which is wrong.
   - "Two on the island": both face the road, mirror images, hoses toward each other.
6. **Small honest things.**
   - Remove the white hairline at the horizon and the stray dashes at night.
   - Lamp caption: an enamel dome after the lamp posts in his Route 66 photo; the 1934 photo has globes.
   - Tag the notice wording PROPOSED.
   - Add to "Plainly": plates and number windows show white in the dark as well as the globes; seen from below the lit lamp is a ring; posts are round where his photo's are square.

Stills I want to see after: the pump at night in his lamp; beside a pump under the canopy by day; the forecourt shade from the east; his shadow. If 2 can't be done quickly, do 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6, say in "Plainly" that the shade lines are too regular and are being reworked, and send.

**Not asked for now, and why:** finer dots at distance, a plainer slab, heavier contour on the canopy, drawn lettering, the desert. All real. All things his answer will steer, and the sheet already says most of them.

## What to ask him, in plain words

1. **Your two pumps.** We've drawn the same short pump twice, mirror images, both facing the road with their hoses toward each other, like the two yellow pumps in your Route 66 photo. One says HI-TEST, one REGULAR, one globe is cracked. Is that right, or do you want two different pumps?
2. **Dots and lines.** You said the light shouldn't redraw a thing. So each thing is drawn once in dots and never changes. Shade, your shadow and the dark at night are drawn in lines, on the ground and the walls. Is that the split you want? Or should shade be dots too, so everything is in one hand?
3. **A pump standing in the canopy's shade.** Should it look shaded at all, or look exactly as it does in the sun, with only the ground and wall behind it shaded?
4. **On your phone.** Walk up to a pump. Do the dots hold still or shimmer? Is it still fluid? Does press and hold only walk now?

Tell him, don't ask: the middle canopy lamp is the one that works unless he says otherwise; LLANO is still a stand-in; this sheet is a test of how things and light are drawn, not a finished picture.

## My picks (for the orchestrator only; not to be told to him first)

- **Pumps:** the same pump twice, mirrored.
- **Light:** lines for the light. It is his own word, and it is the only one of the three that reads as shadow.
- **Pump in shade:** shaded, but barely. Dots first, at any distance.
- **Lamp:** the middle one.

So my guess at what he says next, to be checked against what he does say: yes to the pumps; yes to lines for shade, with a remark that the lines look mechanical or that the ground is plain; and a wish for more black by day.
