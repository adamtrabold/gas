## Where the owner's answers overruled the jam

## The lot

One layout. It comes from where the sign's shadow really falls (a 12 m sign, a generic desert sun), because in a world where ink is shadow that shadow is there whether wanted or not.

```
                               N
   [ ---------------- station, front faces south ---------------- ]
        garage door       window        DOOR      bench
   +--------------------+
   |  canopy            |                 |
   |  W pump    E pump  | air post        |  16 m, open slab
   +--------------------+                 |
                                        [SIGN]                      phone box
   ============================ road, east–west ============================

                    you arrive here, 21 m south of the sign
```

- **The sign stands at the road, straight in front of the door.** It carries his name and nothing else ("Gas is unnecessary"). He wants it **neon and historically accurate** to the article he sent, which is now read (`inspo/sign-article/`): a bulb-studded steel arrow bolted to an older sign. Whether the name is neon or plastic letter boxes, and which arrow, are out with him (`elements.md`, element 5). The arrow points down beside the name; "dives at the door" no longer describes it.
- **You arrive across the road, 21 m south of the sign,** facing it. From there the whole name is in the picture on a phone, and the road lies between you and the place.
- **The canopy and pumps are to the west; the door is clear of them.** This is the director's "door at the edge of the shade", with a reason he didn't have: a canopy anywhere north of the sign catches the sign's shadow on its roof, where nobody can see it. Put to the west, it only catches the morning shadow, and "the shadow goes in under the canopy" is a good thing for a shadow to do.
- The owner has confirmed it: "Canopy over pumps yes." And it is **joined to the building** ("joined"): it comes off the front wall west of the door, as in his abandoned desert station photo.
- **The station is abandoned** ("abandoned"), and the wall is **stucco over brick**. Labels on things are **realistic, with invented brand names**. **The power never went off** ("abandoned can still have power"). What each of these does to each element is in `elements.md`, which is now the list of what the place contains: it adds utility poles and wires along the road, a lamp post on the forecourt, lettering on the window glass, boulders, clouds, and mountains and brush in the distance, all at his word.

## Moving

One world, no stops. Two ways to move, on every device, and they are the same two everywhere.

**Walk and look.**

| | Look | Walk |
|---|---|---|
| Phone | Drag | Hold: you walk toward where you're looking |
| Desktop | Drag, or move the pointer to the edge | W A S D or arrow keys; or hold the mouse button |
| Keyboard only | Left and right arrows turn | Up and down arrows walk |

**Go to.** Tap or click a spot on the ground, or a thing, and you walk there and stop. Every thing has a standing spot and a facing, so going to a thing puts you down in front of it with a picture worth having. This is where the composed views went. It is also the whole of keyboard and screen-reader movement (below), so it isn't an extra.

Rules of walking:

- Eye height, always. You can look up to the top of the sign and the sky, and down to your feet.
- Four metres a second. From where you arrive, the door is 37 m and about nine seconds held; a go-to never takes more than four, however far.
- You slide along walls. You can't get stuck, fall, or be anywhere you can't walk out of.
- Stop, and the view levels itself over a second.
- Motion turned off: walking and go-to become cuts to standing spots.

**Named places** are the old stops, kept as standing spots with names: the road, the forecourt, the door, the counter, the aisle, the far end, the back lot. You never have to use the names. They exist so that anyone can jump (see "Without the picture").

## The four gestures, walked

| Gesture | What you do | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Look | Stand near a thing and face it | No pointing from across the lot. On a phone a pump is 20 pixels wide from the road; anything that matters is worth walking up to |
| Call | Tap or click a thing you're standing at; Enter on the keyboard | Out of reach, the same tap is a go-to. So one tap walks you there and the next one acts |
| Cross | Keep walking into the door for a second | The hold that walks you is the hold that leans on the latch. Tap-and-hold or Enter does the same |
| Wait | Stand still | Below |

## The door, and the inside being bigger

The door is still one act of about a second, answered by the stroke across the screen. That stroke is also what hides the join between two spaces:

- **Outside** is the lot and the desert. The building is 8 m deep. You can walk all the way round it.
- **The front room** is real and the right size. You see it through the window from the lot, and you see the lot through the window from inside.
- **Past the front room, the inside keeps going** further than the back wall you walked round. In the concept it is one aisle, 28 metres long. Nothing explains it. From no single spot can you see both facts at once; you only know because you walked both.
- **The back door** at the far end lets you out behind the building. Walk round the corner and the front is eight metres away.

A link someone sends always lands on the road. Crossing a door is the only thing that adds to the browser's history, so **back puts you outside the last door you came through**, and Escape does the same. Walking never fills the history.

## Where the world ends

The director's proposal, agreed, with a mechanism:

- Past about 100 m, things are lost. That's already a rule of the drawing (far things are lost).
- At about 130 m from the sign, walking away, you are moved to the same distance on the opposite side, **keeping the direction you're facing.** You're now walking toward the sign. Nothing you could see has changed: the ground is bare out there, the hills are at the horizon, and the sun is where it was, so your shadow doesn't jump.
- A few seconds later the top of the sign comes up ahead.
- The centre of the turn-round is the foot of the sign, which is on the road. So someone walking off down the road comes back up the road; someone walking into the desert behind the station arrives from the front.

Any direction, about half a minute out and you're arriving again. Same station, same hour. Nobody can be lost, and "the road" is always in the list of places.

**The desert has to obey the same join.** The far hills are at the horizon and look the same from both sides, so they can stay. Marks on the ground (scrub, a fence line, a cactus) either stop before 100 m, or are laid so that the ground at any spot matches the ground at the opposite spot. Otherwise the scrub at your feet jumps when you're moved.

Also: lights. At night the sign's bulbs would be seen from much further than 100 m, which would show the join. I'd lose lights at the same distance as everything else, and let the bulbs come on as you get near.

## True of the place at any hour

These came out of the folded concepts and belong to the place.

- **The sign's shadow lies on the slab and moves through the day.** With the sign as the article has it, the shadow is the sign's shape: a tall bar with the arrow's hook at the near end, not a clean arrow. It lies toward the pumps in the morning, toward the door at midday, and away east in the afternoon. In summer it's short. Nothing is done with it. It's there to be found and followed.
- **On winter middays it reaches the door** and comes in over the threshold. On the shortest day the top of the sign is on the door.
- **The wall clock swings it.** Turn the clock indoors and, through the window, the shadows go round.
- **At midday the door is off the latch.** The one hour the place lets you in without asking. An extra; nothing depends on it.
- **A landed word is rare.** The counter bell has one (DING), after the owner's HONK. Nothing else does. Switches, latches and steps have their light or their sound and no word.

## Twilight

For about half an hour at each end of the day there's no sun and not yet dark. Lights can only show against ink, so dusk is the sky being inked in from the top while the sign comes on against it.

What someone arriving then sees, so it doesn't look broken:

| | |
|---|---|
| 0 s | The road. The station with no shadows on it, the flattest it ever is. Sky still paper |
| 3 s | A stroke of ink is laid into the top of the sky. The first letter of the name is lit against it. A buzz starts |
| 4–9 s | A stroke and a letter about each second, down the name |
| 10 s | The first bulb of the arrow, and a click |

Whenever in the half hour you arrive, the next letter comes at three seconds. At dawn it runs the other way: the ink is lifted and the letters go out. If the odd things happen by themselves at any time, it's now.

## Wait

Standing still is the gesture. Shared rules:

- **Still** means not walking and not acting. The clock for it starts when the view has levelled.
- **Turning to look does not restart it.** Otherwise you could never watch what the place does. (In the concept, turning brings your lamp back; it still doesn't restart the place's list.)
- **Walking or acting ends it at once.** Anything that was moving by itself stops. Anything that already changed stays changed for the visit: that's the evidence it happened.
- If the page is hidden, the clock stops. Nothing happens while you can't see it; that would be a different kind of site.

The concept says what Wait gives, second by second.

## Adding to the place (jobs 13 and 14; 04 folded in here)

**The world is places, things and cues.** A thing has a spot, a standing spot, a shape, and its answer to Look and Call as an ink mark and a sound. A cue has a *when* (an hour, a date, how long you've stood still) and a *what*.

**Marked out** (from 04). A thing Adam plans and hasn't made is a dashed footprint on the ground and one word in his hand. That's the first step of adding anything: one line saying where and what. You can stand inside the outline of something that isn't there yet. When he makes it, the outline is gone and the thing is there.

**Marks in another hand** (from 04). Among his outlines, a few that aren't his and never get made. Three circles on the back lot. Alien evidence, in every concept.

**No in-between look.** A generated thing is drawn by the same rules as one he made, and nobody is told which is which. The swap is per thing and unseen: his strokes (one sheet replaces the generated marks everywhere), then his Blender model, then his paint on it.

Dropped from 04: the ledger, WET INK cards, the roughed-in look. `04-the-makeover.md` is kept with a line at the top saying so.

## Sound: where to begin

The owner: "yes but idk how to even begin there." And earlier: "we need sound effects at least." So this is a beginning he can change, built the way the strokes are.

**The principle** (the visual designer's, and it's right): every light has a sound, and the place's own sound is the sum of the lights that are on. By day one light, the sun: wind and hot metal. At night the lamps. Under stars alone, almost nothing.

**Three kinds of sound, and where each comes from.**

| Kind | What | Heard |
|---|---|---|
| The bed | The lights that are on | From where each light is. Walk toward the sign and its buzz grows; walk away and it's wind |
| Your own | Footsteps | Under you. Stop walking and they stop, so Wait is something you hear yourself begin |
| Answers and events | The bell, the latch, a switch; and the things the place does by itself | From where the thing is, left or right, near or far. You hear it and turn |

That third line matters more now that you walk. A sound behind you is the plainest reason to turn round, and most of what the place does when you're still should be heard before it's seen.

**Day one: eleven sounds.**

| Sound | For |
|---|---|
| Wind | The sun; the bed by day and the floor under everything |
| Heat tick | Metal in the sun: pumps, canopy |
| Sign buzz | The name and the arrow, lit |
| Relay click | Each bulb as the arrow runs |
| Tube hum | The canopy lamp; the lights indoors |
| Compressor | The cooler |
| Steps on gravel, on concrete, on lino | You, in the desert, on the slab, indoors |
| Latch | The door giving |
| Bell | The door; the counter |

**Generate for now.** Every one of these can be made in the browser from noise and tone, with no recordings: wind is filtered noise; a buzz or hum is a low tone with its overtones; a click or a step is a short burst; a bell is two tones dying away. So sound starts exactly where the strokes start: generated stand-ins, readable, not precise.

**Then his own, one at a time.** Each sound is a named slot. Drop a recording in with that name and it replaces the generated one everywhere it's used; the rest stay generated until he gets to them. Same as the stroke sheet, and as easy.

**One thing to offer a musician.** A hum has a pitch. If each lamp is tuned to one note, the lights that are on make a chord, and the place's sound at any moment is a chord nobody arranged: the sign alone at dusk, more notes as the lamps come on, one note left when you've put the rest out. He picks the notes. It needs no music written, it follows from the rule we already have, and if he doesn't want it the hums are just hums. (The visual designer's offer of a tone that follows the sun's height is the daytime half of the same idea.)

**Rules.** Sound starts with your first touch, never before. One switch, in the margin. Nothing depends on hearing: with sound off, a light that's on shows a short repeating stroke where its hum would be, and anything heard-first leaves a small mark where it happened. Only the counter bell lands a word. Music, and whether the radio plays his, is still "idk yet"; the radio gives static and a station that won't quite come in.

## The swim test (asked for first)

**Result: the ink sits in the world.** Walk past the edge of a shadow or the edge of a lamp's pool and its broken shape stays where it is on the ground, like something painted there. It changes when the light changes and not when you move.

How it was tested. I built a new walked prototype of the whole lot in three.js (`sketches/place/`). The broken edge of every light is made from a pattern that is read at the point in the world each pixel shows, not at the pixel's place on the screen. The pattern is fibres that run the way the light runs: parallel for the sun, outward from a lamp. The test (`sketches/place/swim.js`) follows one fixed spot on the ground through six frames, turning and then walking toward it, cuts the same small square round that spot from each frame, and counts how many pixels changed from one frame to the next. It does this twice: ink fixed to the world, and ink fixed to the screen as in last round's prototype. Picture: `stills/swim-test.png`.

| | Ink fixed to the world | Ink fixed to the screen |
|---|---|---|
| Night, edge of the canopy lamp's pool: turning | 0.4% changed | 4.0% |
| Night, same: walking | 1.2% | 3.5% |
| Day, a shadow edge on the slab: turning | 1.8% | 3.5% |
| Day, same: walking | 3.7% | 5.1% |

What I saw in the frames. At night the pool's edge has the same tongues and the same islands of light in all six frames; they grow as you approach and do nothing else. With the ink fixed to the screen, the edge is a different shape in every frame. By day the gap is smaller because a sun shadow's edge is nearly a clean line, so there is less to swim; the changes that remain in the world version are the square's perspective changing as you walk.

What it does not prove:

- **I have not felt it in the hand.** The test was run in a browser without a screen. I judged from frames and counts. Someone should walk the prototype on a phone.
- **These are fibres of noise, not brush strokes.** The edge breaks in the right direction and stays put, but it has no swell and taper. The owner's own marks would have to be laid along the same fibres. That is the next test, and it needs a sheet of his strokes.
- **Cost.** One extra full-screen pass and a depth read. Untested on a real phone.

One more thing came out of it. To keep paint sharp while light frays, the ink pass has to know which is which. In the prototype, lit surfaces carry a faint tint that paint doesn't have. So "two blacks" is now a mechanism: the door card and the letters on the sign are sharp at any distance, and no shadow edge is.

## Camera at full rate, ink held

Done in the prototype: the view is redrawn every frame. And with the ink fixed to the world there is nothing left to hold while you walk, because nothing swims. "Held" now applies only to things that happen: a word landing, the lamp closing in steps, the light moving on. Same caveat as above: I haven't walked it on a device.

## The rules of the drawing

1. **Ink is where the light isn't.** A sunlit wall against the sky has no edge (`place-day-door`).
2. **Two blacks.** Shadow breaks at its edge and moves with the light. Paint is flat and sharp and never moves: stripes, arrow, lettering, the door card, the garage door.
3. **The light sets the grain.** Sun: parallel. Lamp: outward. Stars: one stroke each, round the still point.
4. **Far things are lost.** Past about 100 m. This is also what hides the end of the world (below).
5. **Dots.** A regular row is bulbs. A loose cluster is the alien material.
6. **Words that land** are slab letters known by the ink they throw (`cross-2d`).

## The building (one, for all three)

I've used the systems designer's lot: sign at the road, door 16 m straight north of it, canopy and pumps to the west.

- **The canopy is back on**, as the director ruled, and **it sits west of the door**, as systems argued. I agree with systems over the steer here. With the canopy in front of the door the sign's shadow would land on its roof, and the door would be in shade all day.

- **Two things I added to the lot,** for the director and systems to accept or not: a shop window east of the door with a low sill (the room needs a view of the slab), and a bench under it. The garage door is paint.
- **At night the canopy is lit from below** (`place-night-canopy`): a bright slab on posts with a pool under it. Nothing has been added to make it strange.

## The sign

A 12 m pylon. The name in seven boxes stacked down the west side. A flat painted arrow hanging beside it, point down, with two rows of bulbs. A starburst on top.

- **Its shadow is an arrow.** Because the arrow is flat and faces the sun, it throws an arrow-shaped shadow on the slab, pointing away from the sign (`02-noon-door`, `02-winter-noon`). That is in every concept, because it's just the sun.
- **From the side the sign is a line** (`01-anywhere`). It is flat. Real ones are too, but this one vanishes. Either the boxes get real depth or the owner is asked; it's on the list below.
- **From the arrival spot the letters are small** on a phone. They're readable at 15 m and not much further.

## Where the world ends

The director asked whether a horizon in two values can hide the join. **Yes.** By day the ground far out is paper and the sky is paper, so there is nothing at the horizon to see jump. At night both are ink. The hills are far enough to be the same from both sides. `place-edge` is the view 86 m out, walking back in: the sign is coming back a few marks at a time.

I agree with systems that lights have to be lost at the same distance as everything else. In the prototype the bulbs are, because they pass through the same test.

## The sign, to check against the owner's saved references

Neither the article nor the photo pool can be opened from this session. For whoever can:

1. **How the arrow is hung.** Ours is a separate flat arrow beside the pole, pointing straight down. Was the arrow of that kind usually a separate piece, part of the panel, or on top of it? Did it point straight down, dive at an angle, or curve over?
2. **Where the bulbs are.** Ours has two rows along the arrow's edges. Were they on the face, on the edge, or outlining the whole sign? Did they chase toward the point?
3. **How the name is carried.** Ours is one letter per lit box, stacked. Were stacked single-letter boxes used on these signs, or is that a motel habit? Would a station more likely have one long lit panel?
4. **The starburst.** Is it of that era and that kind of sign, or a decade later?
5. **Thickness.** How deep was such a sign? Ours disappears from the side.
6. **The pole.** One pole or two; plain or with fins.
