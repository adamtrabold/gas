## 3. Rulings

**Are the four one place?** Yes, and they should be. The owner described one place in detail: a plain Route 66 station, a Googie arrow sign with his name, the desert, nobody there. So there is one station, one sign, one set of drawing rules. That is the foundation, not a failure to differentiate.

What must stay different is what `00-shared.md` already tabulates: **what you are, and what the hour does.** Those are real forks and one of them is the owner's own unanswered question. So: one place, three concepts, and do not blend them this round. Each has to stand whole by itself, with a day, a night and an inside. When they go to the owner he will be told they can be combined; that is his choice to make from things he can see, not ours to make for him by merging early.

**Fold 04?** Yes. It is not a concept, both of you said so, and `04a` shows it isn't liminal. A half-built place has a reason to be empty. What moves into `00-shared.md`:
- **Marked out.** A thing Adam plans and hasn't made is a dashed footprint and one word in his hand. It is how he adds a thing (job 13), and you can stand inside the outline of something that isn't there yet.
- **Marks in another hand.** The three circles. Best alien evidence drawn so far.
- **No "roughed in" look.** A generated thing is drawn by the same rules as a finished one and nobody is told which is which. That is what "generate for now" means.
- **The ledger and WET INK cards:** dropped for now. He can ask for a ledger later; it is one book on a counter.
Delete `04-the-makeover.md` or leave it with a first line saying it is folded and where its parts went.

**Does 02 stand without an inside?** No. Nothing goes forward without an inside. His first sentence is "you start out of the gas station and can go in". 02 gets one round to earn its place, on changed terms:

- **The raked dart is dropped.** A 20 m beam climbing away from the business is apparatus, and it is not the sign in his references: the arrow in the article he saved flashes and dives at the door. One sign in every concept: the upright pylon with TRABOLD in stacked boxes, the arrow diving at the door, as in `01a`.
- **02 becomes: the sign's shadow is the path the day lays.** A tall pylon and arrow throw a long band across the lot morning and evening and a short one at midday. You walk along it and arrive at whatever it ends on. That sentence came from the visual designer and it is the part of the sundial that survived contact with eye height. No joints as a dial, no table of seven things laid out once.
- **Its inside gets a role or the concept folds.** Direction to try: indoors is where you see the dial and work it. The window frames the forecourt and the band on it; the wall clock is in your hand; you turn it and watch the path swing across the lot outside. The sun patch on the floor stops being "the hand indoors". `02d` proved it can't be.
- **Kill condition.** If, walking at eye height on a phone, a visitor can't tell which thing is awake without being told, or the inside still has no job, 02 folds into the shared file as one line ("the sign's shadow is real, long, and worth following") and two concepts go forward. I proposed the sundial. I would sooner fold it than send the owner a weak version of my own idea.

**The canopy.** Put it back, in every concept. Three of his five station pictures have one (01, 03, 09). Without it `01a` doesn't read as a gas station. The shade under a canopy is a threshold you stand in before the door, which is what liminal means. And at night a canopy lit from below is a disc with a pool of light under it, which the visual designer already spotted as alien evidence that needs nothing added. Keep the door readable: canopy over the pump island, door at the edge of its shade or just clear, as in 09. This is still the owner's building, so it goes to him as a question (section 7), but draw it on.

**Pick up.** Dropped until there is something to hold. Walking up to a thing is the handling. Four gestures for now: Look, Cross, Call, Wait.

**The first chart entry being you.** Not on the first visit. A chart that greets you is knowing, and knowing is one step from funny. On your **second** visit there is an entry dated the day of your first, with the place you stood. Someone wrote you down while you weren't here. No sentence.

**12 frames a second.** Fine for ink that is settling. Not for the camera. Walking has to move at the screen's full rate or it will feel broken on a phone; only the ink is held.

## 4. The largest risk: brushwork that swims

He asked for brush strokes first and to walk around second. Right now those two fight: the frayed edges belong to the screen and slide over the world as you move. If that isn't solved, every concept fails for the same reason, so it gets tested before anything else is polished.

Direction: **the ink belongs to the world.** Paint already will, because paint is texture on a surface and that is what he'll export from Blender. Shadow is the hard one. The broken edge of a shadow has to be fixed to the ground or wall it falls on, so that when you walk past it, it stays put like a painted thing, and only changes when the light changes.

## 5. One addition, shared: where the world ends

A walked world needs an edge, and a wall or a fade is the wrong answer here. Proposal: **there isn't one.** Walk away from the station into the desert in any direction and after a while the sign comes up over the horizon ahead of you. You are arriving again. Same station, same hour. This is the loop from the jam, without stops, and it is the plainest form of "liminal" we have: nowhere else exists. It also means a visitor can never be lost. Systems to work it; visual to say whether a horizon in two values can hide the join.
