# Look judge 2 — design director: the station in dots, with real lights

A check before the owner is asked to judge the station again. Not a score. After `owner.md` 30 to 42.

## Verdict: day is ready. Night is not. Three fixes and the controls, then ask.

By day this is the first frame of the project I would call "walking through an illustration". The night, which is what he is about to be asked about, fails his own sentence at a glance: "It should just be darker."

## How I checked

- The designer's thirteen `dots-*` stills at full size.
- The prototype myself, Playwright iPhone 14 descriptor, canvas 1170 × 1992 at 3 dots per point confirmed on every view: `#together`, `#pump`, `#slab` by day, night and night-reversed; `#stipple-together`; the three viewers; `#team-lines-together`. Plus eleven stands of my own: beside a pump, from the east, behind the pumps, looking down at the ground, and turned round to the moon.
- Four of my frames kept as `concepts/stills/lj2-*.png`.
- Limits unchanged: headless, software rendering, stills. Nothing seen moving. Frame rate unknown, and night costs about twice a day frame.

## Would he say…

- **"Posterized"?** No.
- **"Pixellated"?** By day, no. At night, possibly: looking along the ground the screen breaks into rows of half-made dots that read as a glitch, worst in "light dots" (`lj2-night-reversed-ground.png`).
- **"Hard to make out objects"?** No. This is solved.
- **Night wrong at a glance?** Yes, three ways:
  1. **It isn't darker.** From where the link opens, night is the day frame with a black strip of sky. The wall and the whole forecourt are a light screen. In "light dots" the forecourt is nearly white: lighter than "dark dots", and lighter than day shade. He would answer "neither" and the question is wasted.
  2. **Turn round to the moon and the screen is black** with a disc about a sixth of the screen wide (`lj2-night-moon.png`). The list says a quarter. He said "always epic… exaggerated". This is neither, and it is the black screen we promised never to show.
  3. **A white hairline** hangs in the black at the horizon in the pump and slab night views (`lj2-pump-night-hairline.png`).

## Fix first

1. **Night is darker than day at a glance, in both ways of drawing it.**
   - The canopy lamp becomes the lamp in `lights.md`: one weak bulb throwing a pool downward that falls off within a few metres. Not a bare point reaching 14 m.
   - From the opening stand the wall beyond the pool, and the open ground, are plainly dark. The lit things (the pool, the west globe, the plates) are the light parts of the picture.
   - "Light dots" is mostly black, with paper dots gathering where light is.
   - Once that is done, look along the ground at night. If the rows of half-made dots still show at a glance, soften them; if not, leave them and say so.
2. **The moon is epic.** At least the quarter of the screen's width the list promises. Until there is a sky, the frame it sits in is not empty: the moonlit ground and a horizon are in it.
3. **No white hairline at the horizon at night.**
4. **The settled controls go into the station before he is asked anything.** He has said twice that he dislikes press and hold, and once already opened the wrong page and blamed the pinch. If he opens the station to judge night and has to hold to walk, that is what he will talk about. This is a port of work he has confirmed, not design.

I'll look at four stills after: the opening stand at night both ways, the moon, and the ground looked along at night. No full re-check.

**Not fix-first:** the flat black sky above the canopy from the opening stand (it comes with the environment, and he should be told so in half a sentence); sliced dots under the lamp; plates switched by view; twins by day from the road; lettering.

## The one question, and the queue

**First** (the designer's, and I agree):

> Night is now just darker, in dots, with a moon behind you. There are two ways to draw it. Open the link, tap "night, dark dots", then "night, light dots". Which one is night?

**Then, one at a time:**
1. On your phone, moving around: do the dots hold still, and is it still smooth? (Second, because night costs twice a day frame and nothing else matters if it stutters.)
2. Shade as an even printed screen, as now, or as looser hand-made dots? (He offered halftone himself as a "could"; this confirms it. Needs a "stipple" foot link first.)
3. The moon: always full, or tonight's real shape?
4. In the elements page: can you turn a thing with a swipe and still scroll past it?

**Off the queue**
- *Which lamp works:* tell him it's the middle one. He will say if it's wrong.
- *Should a thing's light side follow the sun:* not a question. See below.

## "Drawn once" against real light: ruled

**Real light wins. A thing's light side follows the sun, the moon and the lamps. Don't ask him.**

- "Lighting should work as in the real world" is later, wider, and his own sentence. "No" was one word against a lettered caption on a picture of a lone pump, and our reading of it ("drawn once") was never confirmed by him. A pick outranks an example; a rule outranks a reading.
- He has already applied the rule himself to the nearest case: a thing in the canopy's shade is shaded.
- A pump whose bright side faces away from the moon, under a real moon, is the kind of thing he catches in a glance.
- What stays fixed is matter: rust, stains, pits, cracks, wear, the paper. Those are the thing's own dots and never move. What turns with the light is tone: which side is dark.
- So the split that survives from Sheet 2 is not dots against lines. It is **matter is fixed, light is real**, in one hand.
- If he sees it and objects, it is one switch back. Asking first would spend a question on something he has already answered twice.

Do this with the environment turn, not before the night question; it changes the pump's day look slightly and he should meet it in a whole picture.

## What next

1. **This turn:** fixes 1 to 3 and the controls into the station. Then the night question.
2. **Then the environment, straight away, as two turns.** He asked for it by name and has been waiting since before the lights. First turn: the far ground as solid shapes, horizon, mountains, the sky by day and at night with the moon and stars, the road. One whole picture to stand in, day and night. Second turn: the station front, scrub, boulders, poles. Real light on things goes in with the first.
3. The sign after that; his photos have arrived.

Controls before environment only because it is small and because every judgement he makes from now on happens through them. It must not become a turn of its own.

## For the next director

Private placing: day, the three together, 8.5. Night, 6.5 as built; the drawing is right and the light is wrong. Nothing is a 9 and nothing is offered to him as finished.

# After the night fixes: send, with one small thing first

Checked from the stills asked for (`night-fix-1` to `-4b`); not walked again.

**Night is now night.** From the opening stand the picture is mostly dark, the lamp makes a pool on the island, the lit globe and plates are the bright things, and moonlight comes in under the canopy as a wedge on the wall. That is "just darker", with real light. The moon is big, ringed, and has ground under it. The hairline is reported gone.

**One thing he'd catch, to fix before the link goes (no further look from me):**
- Turned round to the moon, a band of bare paper lies along the horizon under a black sky, where the far ground runs out of dots. At night it reads as a glow or a fault. Far ground at night ends in black, not paper.

**Seen and accepted for a test:**
- In "light dots" the rows on the ground are still there near the island, and the pumps have blobby pale outlines. It is the rougher of the two.
- In "dark dots" the moonlit ground is dense and reads a little like gravel.
- The fascia is a pale band between a black sky and a black underside. True to the moon; odd until there is a sky.

## The two calls

1. **Two nights in two kinds of drawing: accepted for the question.** He is choosing between two pictures, and both are honest pictures. Don't explain the mechanics to him. Two things follow:
   - Whichever he picks has to be brought back to one hand with the day. If he picks dark dots, night's packed dots and day's even screen must be reconciled, and it must be steadied; one point in six changing over half a metre may swim as he pinches in. That is the next job, not a reason to wait.
   - The follow-up question (do the dots hold still, is it smooth) is asked in the night he picked.
2. **"Always epic" does not mean always low.** Epic is its size and that it is always there. "Lighting should work as in the real world" means it crosses the sky and the shadows move with it. Fixing the hour so it sits near the horizon is fine for this test and should be said in "how it works". Whether it should be held low is his to say later, alongside the phase question; don't ask now.

## The question, final wording

> Night is now just darker, in dots, with a big moon behind you (turn round to see it). There are two ways to draw it. Open the link, tap "night, dark dots", then "night, light dots". Which one is night? (The sky and the land around aren't drawn yet; that's next.)

Queue after it, unchanged: holds still and smooth on his phone; even screen or looser dots for shade; the moon's shape, and with it whether it stays low; the 3D views.
