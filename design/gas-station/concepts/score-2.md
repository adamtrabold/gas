## Did the work really change since round 1?

Yes, and in the thing that mattered most.

- **There are brush strokes in the walked world now.** Round 1 was flat fill with ragged edges. `lo-strokes-night`, `lo-inside-lights-b`, `lo-door-in`, `lo-aisle` and `lo-night-lamp` are ink drawings: strokes with a swell and a taper, laid along the light, some of them missing. The lamp pool raying out under the canopy is the first frame in this project that is plainly Kirby without being a comic.
- **They stay on the world.** In my own walk the road's band of strokes and the building's shade kept their shapes as the view moved. The walk-past test's flattened squares agree: same marks from eight stands, different marks in the control.
- **The arrival holds the whole name** at 390 × 844 in the live first frame. I saw "ABOLD" last round; I see TRABOLD, the arrow, the door and both ends of the station now.
- **Near things exist in shade** (`lo-canopy-shade`): the pumps stand under the canopy.
- **The merge is a better concept than either half.** "You always cast a shadow and always carry the lamp; each shows where it can" is cleaner than the day/night switch I asked for. The systems designer went past my steer and was right to. It also means a daytime visitor has the lamp within ten steps of the door, which fixes the thin day without inventing anything.

So the score goes up for work that changed. It doesn't go to 9, for the reasons below.

## The owner's likely objections

Against his own words.

1. **"Gas is unnecessary."** He has already said it, and the blade is in the arrival frames and `lo-sign-side`. As it stands the concept shows him something he has turned down. Certain, and trivial to fix.
2. **"Where's the desert?"** He said "set in the desert". By day there is paper above the station and paper below it and nothing that says desert: no horizon, no hills, no scrub. `lo-anywhere-day` is a blank page with one line. Blank is right for Ruscha's sky. It isn't right for the ground and the horizon. **Likely.** Nobody has raised this before, including me.
3. **"The first thing I see by day isn't brushed."** "Monocolor black and white brush strokes." From the road the day building is small flat shapes; the only brush in the first frame is the road. The strokes are fixed in size in the world, so from 37 m they merge. The visual designer says this plainly. The night arrival doesn't have the problem. **Likely enough to matter**, because the day arrival is what most people will see first.
4. **"It went black."** At night I turned away from the station and the screen was solid ink: no disc, nothing. The lamp found no surface. A visitor who turns round in the first ten seconds at night is holding a black phone.
5. **"What is that shape?"** Your shadow, in `lo-door-in`, has two prongs at the top like ears, and in `lo-strokes-day` a point. He said "liminal not funny". A shadow with ears on a site with aliens in it is a joke nobody meant.
6. **"Where's the time of day stuff?"** He asked for it by name. Day and night are drawn. Twilight, which the files call the best new idea of last round and which is the hinge between "the sun's" and "yours", is words only.
7. **"These look generated."** They do: even, combed, one family of mark. He accepted "generate for now" and the sheet is one file to replace, which is the right answer. Not a blocker.
8. **"How does it feel?"** Nobody has held it. See below.
9. Unlikely to object to: the place, the sign with his name, the canopy, night, the room, the aisle, the lights as sound, leaving lights on, the rule that nothing moves while you look at it.

## Rulings the designers asked for

- **Lamp in daytime shade: no.** The visual designer is right; `lo-canopy-shade-lamp` reads as a fault, and "daylight is the sun's" is the plainest sentence in the concept. The lamp shows where no daylight reaches: indoors and at night. This removes choice 1 from the list for the owner.
- **Arrival across the road, 21 m south of the sign: yes.** It is what makes the name readable on a phone, and the road as a band of strokes is the best brush in the day arrival. Walk at 4 m a second so the door is about nine seconds away.
- **Twilight before it goes to the owner: one frame, yes.** Not the animation. One still of the sky part inked with three or four letters lit against it. He asked for "time of day stuff" by name and it's the hinge of the concept.
- **The shadow: the person's proportions**, as recommended, but see fix 5. I'm withdrawing my request to show him two shapes; the visual designer is right that at brush size it isn't a choice.
- **GAS blade: gone.** His words.
- **"A sunlit wall against the sky has no edge."** Keep. With brushed shade it is now what makes the day building Ruscha. The desert fix below will give it something to sit in.

## Fixes to reach 9

1. **Remove the GAS blade** and re-shoot the arrival frames and `lo-sign-side`. The side view is carried by the depth of the letter cabinets and the arrow's slab.
2. **Put the desert in.** A horizon the whole way round: low mesas or hills as a few long dry strokes, exempt from "far things are lost" because they are the far thing. Scrub as sparse marks on the ground beyond the slab. One or two things from his pictures (the cactus in 09, the fence line in 10) if they earn it. `lo-anywhere-day` should be a picture of a desert with nothing in it, not an empty page. Check the horizon doesn't show the join at the world's edge.
3. **Brush in the day arrival.** From 37 m the canopy's shade, the eaves and the recesses should read as brushed, not as flat shapes. Try a second, larger stroke scale for big masses seen from far off, still fixed to the world, giving way to the finer one as you walk in. If that swims or looks wrong, say so, and the answer becomes the desert marks and the road doing the work.
4. **Never a black screen at night.** The lamp always finds the ground in front of your feet. Turn away from the station and you still have a disc of paper and the strokes in it.
5. **The shadow must not have ears, a hood or a point.** A head and shoulders, cut by the bottom of the screen, readable as the person holding the phone at the door (`lo-door-in`) and on the slab. If the brush eats it into prongs, make the shadow the one mark that is less ragged than the rest.
6. **Twilight, one frame.**
7. **Update the how-it-works half** to match: lamp indoors and at night only; arrival across the road; walk speed; canopy confirmed; no GAS; the list of choices for the owner is now the tuned hums and nothing else.
8. **The look back down the aisle:** solve it (the front room dark enough that the window is the bright thing) or take the claim out of the file. Don't leave it promised and unbuilt.
9. **Make the prototype safe to hand him.** A way to drop the drawing resolution if the frame rate is poor, the debug caption off the picture, and whatever else stops a slow phone being his first impression.

Fixes 1, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9 are small. 2 and 3 are the real work, and they're both the visual designer's.

## Does the missing phone walk block a 9?

Not by itself. This is the concept phase; the score is for the idea and its fit to what he asked, and the idea is proven as far as frames can prove it. The owner walking it on his own phone **can be the phone test**, on two conditions:

- It goes to him described as what it is: crude boxes, generated strokes, no sound, a test of how walking in ink feels.
- Fix 9 is done first, so a slow frame rate degrades instead of failing.

What I can't promise him is that it runs well. If it doesn't, that is a finding about the method (one read of the sheet and eight shadow reads per pixel) and it comes back to the team as the first job of the build, not as a reason the concept was wrong.

## What keeps blocking a 9 across the two rounds, and why

1. **The look arrives one round behind the idea.** Round 1: the idea was there and nothing was brushed. Round 2: it's brushed, and now the day arrival and the desert are what's missing. Each round the visual work has gone to the hardest technical question (does ink stay put; can strokes live in the world), rightly, and what a first-time visitor sees in the first three seconds by day has been last in the queue both times.
2. **I didn't hold the pictures against "set in the desert" until now.** It's in his first paragraph. That's my miss, in both rounds.
3. **A lot of the concept is still words.** Lights arriving in strokes, the crossing, the standing-still list, most of the alien material. That's acceptable for a concept and I haven't marked it down, but it's why the owner's own walk matters: he will meet the parts that exist.
4. **No device.** Both rounds. About to be answered by him.
