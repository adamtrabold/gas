## The owner's likely objections, before any score

His own words are the test. In the order I think he'd raise them:

1. **"Where are the brush strokes?"** His second sentence was "monocolor black and white brush strokes". Every walked frame is flat black fill with ragged edges. Not one stroke with a swell and a taper. He accepted "generate for now", which covers whose hand it is. It doesn't cover there being no strokes. **Likely, and it applies to both concepts.** This alone keeps both under 9.
2. **"I haven't felt it."** He said "walk around". Nobody has walked this on a phone. Likely to matter the moment he opens it.
3. **"What do I do by day?"** (03) Look down at a shadow; stand next to an air post.
4. **"What do I do at all?"** (01) Tap things, and wait. Its best material needs 15 seconds to five minutes of standing still.
5. **"Is that a tombstone?"** (03) The round-topped column chosen so as not to be a person. He may want a person. The visual designer flags this too.
6. **"That isn't how those signs were."** Unchecked against the article and photo pool he saved. From the side the sign is a line.
7. **"I can't see the pumps."** Under the canopy by day everything is one black.
8. **"Does it need a canopy?"** Asked, not answered.
9. He is unlikely to object to: the place itself, night, the arrow sign with his name, the aisle, the edge of the world, the sound plan. Those are the strong parts.

## 01 Nobody's here — 7

**Frames:** `place-day-road`, `place-day-door`, `place-night-road`, `place-night-canopy`, `01-counter`, `01-aisle-near`, `01-aisle-far`, `01-disc`, `01-anywhere`, `01-wide`, `cross-2d`.

**What's right.** It is the closest to what he literally asked for: a liminal station in the desert, nobody there, you start outside and go in, odd things at odd hours. The aisle that is 28 metres long behind an 8 metre building is the best single idea for "liminal" in the whole work and it only exists because you walk. "Nothing ever moves while you're looking straight at it" is the right rule and keeps it from being a haunted house. The chart writing you down on your second visit is good. A conventional answer isn't marked down for being conventional; this is the right place.

**Why it's a 7.** "Every interaction has artistic meaning" is his sentence, and here the visitor's whole hand is a tap. Call means something (you ask, nobody comes), but it's one meaning repeated across six things. Everything else that makes the concept is the place doing things while you stand still, and the systems designer says plainly that if people don't stand still, it's a shop. That is a bet on visitor patience carrying the idea. By night it has nothing of its own; it is the shared night.

**Can it get to 9?** Not as a separate concept. To get there it needs to give the visitor a hand that means something, and the hand that fits this world is 03's. Its room, its aisle, its stillness and its chart should go into 03. See fix 3 under 03.

**Fixes if it were kept apart** (they also apply once folded in):
1. Counter tube lights the counter only, so the front window is the bright thing from the far end of the aisle.
2. A light at the mouth of the aisle, visible from the door.
3. First rung at 15 seconds, not 40.
4. The far end has to be a picture: the payphone and back door are too small to see in `01-aisle-far`.

## 03 Your light — 8

**Frames:** `place-day-road`, `03-shadow`, `03-touch`, `03-inside`, `03-night-arrive`, `03-night-aim`, `03-night-kept`, `03-other`, `03-anywhere`, `03-wide`.

**What's right.** It takes the one rule the whole look is built on, ink is where the light isn't, and makes the visitor part of it. So every act is ink moving, with no interface layer to design, and "every interaction has artistic meaning" is true by construction: you can only put light somewhere or take it away. It wasn't in the jam and it isn't the idea anyone would have first. Standing still at night gives you the sky, which is the best "do nothing" we have. The room's drawing and the room's sound being the same sum of lights is the answer to "idk how to even begin" with sound, and a musician can tune it. Lights you left on, seen from the road, and one you didn't leave on: that is the alien material with no alien. `03-inside`, the doorway on the floor with your shadow in it, is the strongest single picture of what a visitor is here. The fourth light costs no new art.

**Why it isn't a 9.**
- Objection 1, no strokes, applies in full.
- Its day is thin and both designers say so. By day your shadow can join with the air post, the phone box, the door and the wall. The pumps are in shade. "Your reach is the hour" is true and small.
- By day indoors, the room is black until you find a cord. The cord in the door's light fixes the first one; the rest of the room by day is unproven and undrawn.
- It leaves out the best of 01 for no reason.
- No version without the picture, said honestly.

**Can it get to 9?** Yes. Numbered fixes:

1. **Strokes in the walked world.** Lay generated strokes, with a swell and a taper and some of them missing, along the world-fixed fibres that already exist, so a shadow is a stack of strokes that stays on the ground when you walk. One day frame and one night frame from the walked prototype, plus the same walk-past test. This is the fix for both concepts and the first thing to do. If it can't be done in real time, say so now; that would be a decision for the owner about "threejs" against "brush strokes", and he should get it early.
2. **Accept the day for what it is and say it that way.** Stop trying to make the shadow a second hand. By day the place is whole, lit by one sun, and indifferent to you; you are only a shadow on it and you use things by walking up to them. At night it exists where you put light. Rest is Ruscha, contact is yours. That is a shape, not a weakness, as long as the file says it instead of apologising for it. Drop "come at things from the sunny side" as a rule; keep it as something that's simply true.
3. **Take in 01.** One concept. The attendant's things in the front room; the aisle with its cords; Call answered by nobody; the chart. And the stillness joins up: at night the lamp closes, the sky comes up, and then, if you keep standing, the place starts its own list. By day the list starts at 15 seconds. Keep "nothing moves while you're looking at it".
4. **The paper line for near things in shade.** Build it, so the pumps exist under the canopy by day.
5. **The shadow's shape.** Draw it two ways side by side, the plain column and a plain standing person cut by the bottom of the screen, for the owner to pick. Not hooded, not posed.
6. **Arrival framing at real phone sizes.** The whole name readable at 390 × 844 and at 360 × 640, live, not only in a still.
7. **The sign side-on.** Give the letter boxes real depth so it doesn't vanish from the east.
8. **Landed words are rare.** The bell only. Switches get light and sound.
9. **Inside by day with two or three lights on.** One frame. It's the state most people will spend their time in and nobody has drawn it.
10. **Twilight** as written (sky inked in from the top, the letters one at a time), drawn once, and moved to the shared file.
