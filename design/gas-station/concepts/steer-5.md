# Steer 5 — design director: Left on without the lamp

After `owner.md` sections 19 to 28. A ruling on the concept, not a score. For the systems designer (who is listing dependencies in `05-left-on.md` and `controls.md`), the visual designer (Sheet 3) and the orchestrator (the one question).

His words:

> "Also I don't like the light thing at night. It should just be darker. More dots or maybe reversed dot approach on a dark background."
> Which light? "The light that follows where I look"

## The ruling

**The lamp is out. Left on survives as a concept, and is nearer its own name without it.** This does change the concept as he approved it in idea, so it goes to him as one question. Until he answers, build as if the answer were the plainer option (nothing replaces the lamp); the other option only adds to it.

Why it survives:
- The name was always for three things: the place was left with things on; you leave lights on; sometimes one is on that nobody left. None of the three is the lamp.
- The rule was "every act you can make puts light somewhere or takes it away". Still true: by day you take light away (your shadow), indoors and at night you add it (what you switch on).
- The lamp did two jobs. As a mark of you, it was the weaker of the two. As a guarantee that the screen is never black, it was doing the drawing's job. That job now goes to the drawing: the dark itself is drawn.
- A disc that follows your view is a torch from a game. In "walking through an illustration" it was the one thing laid over the picture instead of being in it. I should have seen that.

## 2. Does it stand as a concept without a second mark?

Yes, but it is lopsided, and he should choose whether that is right.

By day you are a shadow everywhere you walk in sun. At night outdoors, and indoors until you pull a cord, you are nothing: a visitor in a dark illustration who can ring a bell, roll a pump's numbers and go in. Your night mark is delayed and left behind: the lights you turned on, still on when you look back from the road, still on next week. That is the title, and it may be all he wants ("it should just be darker").

**Options, differing in kind**

- **A. Nothing replaces it.** By day a shadow; in the dark, unmarked. What you switch on is your mark and it outlasts you. Plainest; exactly his words; nothing new to build. Risk: outdoors at night there is nothing of you until you've been inside.
- **B. You are a shadow in any light.** The same one mark at every hour. By day the sun casts it. At night the canopy lamp casts it, the lit window casts it across the forecourt, and indoors the tube you just switched on casts it on the floor. So switching a light on is also what makes you appear. Nothing follows your view; nothing is carried; a shadow under a lamp is an ordinary thing in a picture. Cost: shadows from a handful of fixed lamps, in dots.
- **C. You are heard.** No mark at night at all; your footsteps on concrete, gravel, linoleum, and the place's sounds answering. Depends on sound nobody has begun, and silent phones get nothing.
- **D. The dark opens with time.** The longer you stay out of the light, the more of the dark drawing there is, as eyes adjust. It is about you without pointing at you. But it competes with standing still, and it is slow to notice.
- **Ruled out:** anything that follows where you look (dots gathering where you point, a faint cone, a dwell effect). That is the thing he turned down under another name.

**My recommendation: B, on top of A.** It makes one mark instead of two, it joins "your shadow" to "left on" (a light you leave on is a place you'd have a shadow), and it gives every lamp in the place a second use without adding a thing to the list. Held to my own tests: it is felt by someone who never works it out; it is not funny; it is in the picture, not over it. And if he says A, nothing built for A is wasted.

I am wary that this is the clever option and he has just taken the plain one twice. So A is offered first and fairly.

## 4. What sections 19 to 28 change in my reading of his eye

- **Four misses now, and they are not all the same kind.** Sheet 1: the director picked the cut hand and one paper (too plain). Sheet 2: the team and I picked lines for shade; the concept carried the lamp (too systematic). The common fault is not plain or rich. We choose what is easiest to justify in words: a rule with two hands that each mean something, a mark for each half of the day. He chooses what looks like one drawing.
- **One hand, one picture.** "The world should look like walking through an illustration." Anything that reads as a layer over the picture, or as a second system inside it, goes. Test for every device from now on: would it be in the illustration if nobody could touch it?
- **I mis-weighted his own sentence.** I took "shadow methods like crosshatching" as licence for ruled lines. "Like… etc" was an example of making tone from marks, not a choice of mark. When he has since picked (dots), the pick outranks the earlier example.
- **He reasons from the real thing.** "That's how it'd be irl… the same model just worn naturally separately." Night "should just be darker". Controls "like photos". When unsure, ask what the real place, or the familiar gesture, would do.
- **Halftone.** Offered, not ordered. It opens a regular screen as a kind of dot, which his brush-pen flash sheet and printed comics both have, and it may be what makes large dark areas and night hold together where loose stipple reads as spatter. Sheet 3 should show stipple and a halftone side by side on one shaded wall.
- **Night is a look problem now.** With no lamp, the dark must be a drawing you can walk: darker by more dots, or light dots on a dark ground. The lit things then matter more, not less. Never a black screen is still the bar.
- **He is waiting for the place.** "When are we getting that." Parts have run long. Sheet 4 should be a whole picture he can stand in, not six pieces of ground.
- **One question at a time; the page does the showing.** Sheets are short web pages, drawings as images, text as text. The "Plainly" block stays but must be brief.
- **My own guess was wrong.** I wrote that he would say yes to lines for shade. He said dots in one word.
