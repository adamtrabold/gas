# 04 — The makeover

*How it works (systems). Read `00-shared.md` first.*

The owner: "I always wanted to own an old gas station to make into something." And: "draw but generate for now. I want to eventually export blender models and texture them." This concept takes those two sentences as the idea. The station is in the middle of being made into something, it shows, and you can't quite tell whether it's being restored or left.

**Draws on:** the director's "The board" (showing the working; the drawing more finished each visit); the Kirby originals in the owner's images, where pencil, notes and ink are on the same sheet; "attention is the inker" (visual, pass 1), with Adam as the inker and not the visitor.

**Kept:** the working is visible. The place is more finished each time you come.
**Changed:** The board was flat sheets, the least 3D direction. This is a full 3D place; only the state of finish is on show.
**Dropped:** flats, panels, tiers of time.

**Said plainly: this may not be a concept.** It differs from 01–03 in which clock matters (months, not hours), but most of it could sit on top of any of them. I'm putting it up so the director can kill it, fold it, or keep it. I'd fold it into whichever of the others survives.

## Structure

The stops of 01. What's different is that every thing is visibly in one of three states, and the state is real — it is the same field that says how finished the thing is in the build (job 14).

| State | What it is in the build | How it's seen |
|---|---|---|
| Marked out | Planned; nothing made | A chalk outline on the ground or wall where it will go, with a word beside it in his hand |
| Roughed in | Generated shape, generated strokes | Drawn, but in thin broken line with the construction showing |
| Done | His own model and strokes | Full ink |

Nothing is faked. If the pump is generated, it looks roughed in. When he replaces it, it's done, everywhere, that day.

## The ledger

On the counter, a ledger in his hand. One line per change, dated: "hung the sign." "west pump." It is filled in by the same act that adds or finishes a thing, so it can't drift from the truth.

For a visitor it's the reason to come back (job 10): the place has changed because someone worked on it, not because of a trick. Things changed since your last visit carry a small WET INK card for a while.

For Adam it's the record of the thing he always wanted to do.

## The gestures here

As 01, with one difference: what a thing does depends on its state.

| State | Look | Call / Pick up |
|---|---|---|
| Marked out | The outline holds; the word reads | Nothing. Your hand goes through. A dull sound |
| Roughed in | It takes its blacks, roughly | It answers, plainly |
| Done | Full | Full, with its own sound |

So "every interaction has artistic meaning" is, here: the place answers you exactly as much as it has been made.

## The hour

Light and sound only. The clock this concept cares about is the calendar.

## Doing nothing

The place carries on being worked on. A sound of something being done round the back. Go round, and nobody's there; a brush is wet.

## The alien material

Outlines that aren't his. Among the chalk marks for things planned, a few that are not in the ledger, in a different hand: three circles on the back lot; a shape on the roof. Marked out, never made.

## Adam makes it into something

This is the whole concept. Mark a thing out (one line: where, and a word). Rough it in (generated). Finish it (his model, his strokes). Each step is one change and one ledger line.

## States particular to this concept

- **Day one, almost everything roughed in or marked out:** must read as a place being worked on and not as a broken site. The arrow sign and the door should be done first, so jobs 1–3 are always whole.
- **Everything done:** the concept has used itself up. The ledger remains; new outlines can always appear. But it's a real end, and worth knowing.
- **Nothing remembered:** no WET INK cards; the ledger still has its dates.

## What it has to prove

That it isn't an excuse for being unfinished. That thin broken line under "pure ink" can look deliberate. That it's still liminal and not a building site with a sign saying so.

## Not sure about

- Whether the owner wants the stand-ins seen as stand-ins at all. He may want the generated version to look finished until he replaces it. One question to him would settle whether this lives.
