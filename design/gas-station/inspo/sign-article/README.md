# The owner's sign reference

Source: Beth Mathews, "The History Behind the Signs Lighting Up Our Daily Lives: Vacuum-Form Signage and Human Connection" (Substack).
https://bethmathews.substack.com/p/the-history-behind-the-signs-lighting

The owner gave this as the reference for the sign: "Re sign and name it should be neon and historically accurate [link] and also maybe some of the letters could flicker" (`owner.md` section 16). ## What the article says (facts, with its own words where short)

- **Before 1900:** hand-painted wooden or metal signboards.
- **Around 1900–1920s:** electric signs, "bright bulbs lighting up Broadway theaters and downtowns".
- **Neon:** arrived in the US in the 1920s, "considered a luxury", "remaining popular until the early 1950s".
- **Early 1950s on:** vacuum-formed (thermoformed) plastic signs — a heated plastic sheet drawn over a mould; "a 3-dimensional plastic sign with embossed letters", lit from inside. Also called pan-faced signs, Signtronix, Dynalites.
- **1958:** Conrad Escalante and Kozy Boren start Superior Outdoor Display Co. in Long Beach, California.
- **The "Superior arrow":** "an electric, lit-up arrow designed to attach to an existing sign", for small businesses "that couldn't afford to update their whole exterior signage". Described as "flashing". The article does not say the bulbs chase in sequence.
  - 04: Escalante's "Illuminable Sign" patent drawing, 1961, beside a real one — a bent steel channel arrow studded with bulbs, bolted to the side of an older box sign, pointing down and in.
  - 05: a second Escalante patent, 1963 — a bulb-studded arrow that appears to pass through a round sign: a notched tail above, a triangular head below.
- **1964:** Boren buys Gulf Development (later Signtronix) and makes cheap signs for "Mom & Pop" businesses; generic wording so a sign survives a change of owner ("BAKERY", "GROCERY COLD BEER").
- **Dynalite (introduced 1964, heyday 1970–1980):** "a small rectangular sign with rounded edges approximately 2' x 4'".
- **"Spell-Out":** "Dynalite signs installed sideways with each face containing a single letter" — 08 shows MOTEL spelled this way, one letter to a rounded plastic box, on a rusted pipe frame, with an older neon VACANCY sign beside it and plastic boxes above and below.
- **Big Sig (introduced 1978):** a larger 4' x 5' vacuum-formed sign with curved sides.

## What this means for our sign (orchestrator's notes for the team, not the owner's words and not a decision)

- The article is mostly about lit plastic signs, not neon. By its own timeline neon is the older technology (1920s to early 1950s) and the Superior arrow (1958 onward) was made to be bolted onto a sign that already existed. So "neon" and "accurate to the article" are not in conflict: an older neon name sign with a later bulb arrow added is exactly the article's story. Whether that is what he wants is his call.
- Our list's "TRABOLD, one letter to a box, stacked" matches the article's "Spell-Out" (1964 onward) — but that is plastic, not neon. He accepted that item before saying "neon". The two need squaring with him.
- 08 shows neon and plastic boxes living on one frame.
- The article has nothing on how these signs age or flicker.
